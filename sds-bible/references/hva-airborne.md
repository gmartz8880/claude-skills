# Hazard vulnerability assessment for airborne releases

Read when the substance is a gas, vapor, dust, fume, mist, or fiber.

1. Characterize airborne behavior: physical state, vapor density (>1 settles in pits, trenches, basements, confined spaces; <1 rises), vapor pressure / evaporation rate, LEL/UEL or combustible dust Kst/MEC (NFPA 652/654, OSHA Combustible Dust NEP), odor threshold vs. OEL (warning property adequate?), IDLH.
2. List 6–10 release scenarios for both general industry and construction settings, e.g. container/cylinder leak, spill in poorly ventilated room, release into confined space or excavation, hot work ignition, transfer/static discharge, dust cloud from cutting/grinding/sweeping, fire/thermal decomposition, transport incident, cylinder BLEVE, chronic low-level overexposure.
3. Score each 1–5 for Likelihood, Consequence (life safety, fire/explosion, health, environment), and Vulnerability (detection, ventilation, controls in place). Risk = L × C × V (max 125): Low 1–20 (green), Moderate 21–50 (yellow), High 51–90 (orange), Extreme 91–125 (red). Also show the ISO 31000 5×5 score (L × C, max 25: Low 1–4, Medium 5–9, High 10–15, Critical 16–25).
4. For each scenario, give a concrete control: monitoring (4-gas meter, PID, LEL alarm, dust sampling), ventilation, isolation distance (ERG guide), PPE/respirator level, emergency response, and the governing 1910, 1926, and consensus citation.
5. Table columns: Scenario | Setting (GI/Construction/Both) | L | C | V | Risk (L×C×V) | Level | 5×5 Score | Rating | Controls & Citations. Sort by Risk descending.
6. Add an "Emergency planning distances" line from the DOT ERG guide (initial isolation and protective action distances) when the substance is in the ERG.
